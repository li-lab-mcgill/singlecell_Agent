## Summary
Introduces the weighted‑nearest neighbor (WNN) framework that learns per‑cell modality weights to build an integrated neighbor graph for multimodal single‑cell analysis. Demonstrated on CITE‑seq datasets (8,617 cord blood cells; 211,000 PBMCs with 228 antibodies), WNN improves resolution of cellular diversity, enables discovery/validation of new lymphoid subpopulations, and supports rapid mapping of new datasets for interpretable immune-response analyses.

## Hypothesis framed
Per‑cell learned weights that quantify the relative information content of each modality enable superior integrated embeddings and clustering of multimodal single‑cell data compared with single‑modality analyses or naïve concatenation.

## Questions answered
- Does WNN improve resolution of cellular states compared with single‑modality analyses on multimodal CITE‑seq data?
- Can WNN be used to map new datasets to a multimodal reference for rapid cell‑type annotation and interpretation?
- What modality or data quality limitations affect WNN performance (e.g., incomplete/low‑specificity antibody panels)?

## Key findings
WNN produced integrated embeddings that resolved more cellular diversity than RNA‑only or protein‑only analyses; applied to a 211,000‑cell PBMC CITE‑seq atlas (228 antibodies) it enabled identification and validation of previously unreported lymphoid subpopulations, revealed heterogeneous integrin expression among circulating memory T cells, uncovered a gradient of adaptive‑like NK cell states, and identified tight clonal clustering within effector/cytotoxic compartments. Method validated on an 8,617‑cell cord blood CITE‑seq dataset and shown to support rapid mapping of new datasets and interpretation of vaccination and COVID‑19 immune responses.

## Methods used
Weighted Nearest Neighbor algorithm: construct modality‑specific neighbor graphs (per modality embeddings), compute per‑cell modality weights quantifying relative utility, integrate weights into a combined weighted neighbor graph for downstream UMAP visualization, clustering, and trajectory inference; RNA preprocessing examples used SCTransform and PCA; empirical comparisons versus single‑modality analyses and mapping workflows for reference projection.

## Method and dataset
WNN applied to paired multimodal single‑cell CITE‑seq data (RNA + surface protein) including an 8,617 cell cord blood dataset and a 211,000 cell human PBMC atlas profiled with 228 antibodies; experimental design: unsupervised integration and clustering with downstream biological validation. Method assumptions: modalities provide complementary information, per‑cell modality informativeness can be estimated from modality‑specific neighborhood structure, and input embeddings (e.g., PCA for RNA) are appropriate summaries of modality signal.

## Limitations
Performance depends on quality, completeness, and specificity of input modalities (e.g., antibody panel coverage); missing or low‑specificity antibodies shift reliance to RNA and can limit resolution. WNN requires careful handling of modality‑specific noise and batch effects and may need tuning for additional or very different modalities (e.g., RNA+ATAC). The paper does not provide ATAC‑specific preprocessing defaults (TF‑IDF/LSI), explicit depth‑correction strategies for scATAC, quantitative minimum data requirements (cell counts, reads/fragments per cell), formal statistical distributional assumptions, or detailed hyperparameter guidance (k, dimensions per modality) for joint RNA+ATAC WNN.

## Boundary conditions
Works when: Demonstrated on paired RNA+protein CITE‑seq data with datasets from ~8.6k to 211k cells and antibody panels up to 228 markers; works when each modality provides measurable signal (RNA embeddings via SCTransform/PCA were used) and when protein panels contain specific informative antibodies. Works when modality‑specific neighbor graphs reflect biological similarity and when batch effects or modality noise are addressed during preprocessing.
Fails when: Fails or degrades when one or more modalities have very low signal or many nonspecific/missing features (e.g., sparse or low‑specificity antibody panels), when modality‑specific batch effects are not corrected, when modalities require distinct preprocessing not applied (e.g., ATAC needing TF‑IDF/LSI which was not specified), or when computational resources are insufficient for very large, highly multimodal datasets; quantitative thresholds for reliable performance (min cells, reads/fragments per cell) are not provided.
