## Summary
This paper developed SHARE-seq, a split-pool single-cell multiome assay that jointly measures chromatin accessibility and RNA expression in the same cell or nucleus. Using 34,774 paired profiles from mouse skin, it inferred cis-regulatory interactions, defined domains of regulatory chromatin, and introduced chromatin potential to quantify lineage priming from discordance between chromatin accessibility and gene expression.

## Hypothesis framed
Joint measurement of chromatin accessibility and RNA expression in the same single cells can reveal cis-regulatory links and show whether chromatin accessibility changes precede transcriptional changes during lineage commitment.

## Questions answered
- Can paired single-cell RNA and ATAC profiles from the same cells be used to infer cis-regulatory interactions between accessible chromatin regions and gene expression?
- Do accessibility changes at domains of regulatory chromatin occur before corresponding gene-expression changes during mouse hair follicle lineage commitment?
- Can chromatin accessibility state be used to predict future transcriptional state and cell fate outcomes?

## Key findings
SHARE-seq generated 34,774 joint chromatin-accessibility and transcriptome profiles from mouse skin. Co-variation between accessibility and expression identified cis-regulatory interactions and domains of regulatory chromatin that significantly overlapped with super-enhancers. During hair follicle differentiation, accessibility changes at domains of regulatory chromatin often preceded corresponding gene-expression changes, supporting chromatin accessibility as a lineage-priming signal. Chromatin potential predicted future transcriptional states and cell fate outcomes from chromatin state.

## Methods used
Simultaneous high-throughput ATAC and RNA expression with sequencing using split-pool barcoding of fixed cells or nuclei; mixed human and mouse cell-line validation for species separation and doublet assessment; joint analysis of paired ATAC and RNA profiles from mouse skin; co-variation analysis between accessible chromatin regions and gene expression to infer cis-regulatory interactions; identification of domains of regulatory chromatin; comparison of domains of regulatory chromatin with super-enhancers; modeling of chromatin potential from discrepancies between chromatin accessibility and RNA expression.

## Method and dataset
The method is SHARE-seq applied to paired single-cell chromatin accessibility and RNA-expression data from the same cells, including 34,774 joint profiles from mouse skin and mixed human-mouse cell-line validation data. The computational peak-to-gene strategy uses co-variation between chromatin accessibility and gene expression across jointly profiled cells to infer cis-regulatory interactions, assuming that correlated accessibility of nearby regulatory elements and target-gene expression reflects cis regulation, that same-cell pairing reduces ambiguity compared with separately profiled modalities, and that accessibility changes can represent priming before RNA changes.

## Limitations
The summary reports limited benchmarking against existing multiomic or peak-to-gene linkage methods. The main biological demonstration focuses on mouse skin and hair follicle differentiation, so generalizability to other tissues, organisms, disease contexts, and human brain samples requires further validation. Inferred enhancer-gene links and chromatin potential are computational predictions and were not reported here as experimentally perturbed causal regulatory relationships. The approach depends on sufficient sequencing depth and quality for both RNA and ATAC modalities, and chromatin accessibility does not directly measure transcription factor binding, histone modifications, three-dimensional chromatin contacts, or protein abundance.

## Evidence pattern
Entity definition: SHARE-seq, domains of regulatory chromatin, and chromatin potential were defined. Statistical unit: individual jointly profiled single cells from mouse skin. Analysis used: same-cell RNA-plus-ATAC integration, co-variation of accessibility and gene expression, DORC identification, super-enhancer overlap, and chromatin-potential modeling. Validation: mixed-species human-mouse cell-line experiment showed species-specific separation and low doublet rate; biological validation used temporal ordering of accessibility and expression changes during hair follicle differentiation. Boundary conditions: conclusions were shown primarily in mouse skin with paired RNA and ATAC measurements.

## Boundary conditions
Works when: Works when RNA expression and chromatin accessibility are measured in the same single cells or nuclei, enough cells and sequencing depth are available to estimate accessibility-expression co-variation, and the biological process contains asynchronous differentiation states that allow ordering of chromatin and RNA changes. Demonstrated on 34,774 paired mouse skin profiles and mixed human-mouse cell-line validation.
Fails when: May fail or produce unreliable links when ATAC or RNA data are too sparse to estimate co-variation, when regulatory effects are not reflected by accessibility-expression correlation, when distal regulation is mediated through mechanisms not captured by local accessibility, when cell-state composition is insufficient to observe differentiation transitions, or when causal enhancer-gene relationships require perturbation, chromatin-contact, histone-mark, transcription-factor-binding, or protein-level evidence not present in SHARE-seq data.
