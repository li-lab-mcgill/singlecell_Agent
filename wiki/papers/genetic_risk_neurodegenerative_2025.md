## Summary
This paper integrates human brain cell-type-specific enhancer-to-promoter chromatin interaction maps with GWAS summary statistics to assign noncoding neurodegenerative and psychiatric disease risk variants to likely target genes. It finds that neurodegenerative disease heritability, including Alzheimer's disease risk, is enriched in microglial chromatin contact regions, whereas schizophrenia heritability is strongest in neuronal chromatin contacts.

## Hypothesis framed
Common noncoding variants associated with neurodegenerative and psychiatric disorders act through cell-type-specific distal regulatory elements, and enhancer-to-promoter chromatin contacts can identify the relevant target genes and pathways in microglia, neurons, and oligodendrocytes.

## Questions answered
- Are GWAS heritability signals for Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders enriched in microglial, neuronal, or oligodendrocyte chromatin contact regions?
- Can cell-type-specific enhancer-to-promoter interactome maps assign noncoding GWAS variants for Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and schizophrenia to predicted risk genes?
- Are microglial disease-risk genes and pathways shared across neurodegenerative diseases or specific to individual disorders?

## Key findings
Promoter-to-enhancer loops were the most common chromatin interaction class in human cortical microglia, neurons, and oligodendrocytes, and most promoters contacted multiple enhancers. Stratified LD score regression showed that genetic heritability for multiple neurodegenerative diseases was enriched at microglial chromatin contact sites, while schizophrenia heritability was predominantly enriched at neuronal contacts and secondarily at oligodendrocyte contacts. H-MAGMA identified predicted risk genes for Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and schizophrenia; neurodegenerative disease risk genes were overrepresented in microglia compared with neurons and oligodendrocytes, and the implicated microglial genes and pathways were largely disease-specific rather than broadly shared.

## Methods used
H3K4me3-anchored PLAC-seq chromatin interaction mapping in human cortical microglia, neurons, and oligodendrocytes; integration with H3K27ac, H3K4me3, and ATAC-seq annotations; classification of chromatin loops into promoter-enhancer, promoter-promoter, promoter-ATAC, and other categories; stratified linkage disequilibrium score regression to estimate disease heritability enrichment in cell-type-specific contact regions; Hi-C-coupled MAGMA to map GWAS risk variants to genes through chromatin contacts and test disease-associated genes and pathways.

## Method and dataset
The study used human cortical microglia, neurons, and oligodendrocyte chromatin interaction datasets generated by H3K4me3-anchored PLAC-seq, combined with epigenomic annotations from H3K27ac, H3K4me3, and ATAC-seq and GWAS summary statistics for Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and schizophrenia. The number of cortical samples and number of chromatin contacts are not specified in the summary. The analysis assumes that cell-type-specific chromatin contacts can connect noncoding GWAS variants and distal regulatory elements to their target promoters and that GWAS heritability enrichment in annotated contact regions reflects disease-relevant regulatory mechanisms.

## Limitations
Chromatin interaction maps came from a limited set of human cortical samples, including pediatric epilepsy resections, and may not fully represent adult, aging, disease-affected, or brain-region-specific regulatory states relevant to neurodegeneration. The analysis focused on microglia, neurons, and oligodendrocytes and may miss astrocytes, endothelial cells, peripheral immune cells, dopaminergic neurons, motor neurons, and finer microglial or neuronal subtypes. Enhancer-target and GWAS risk-gene assignments are computational predictions based on chromatin contacts and GWAS summary statistics and require experimental validation. GWAS power and ancestry composition may affect which loci, genes, pathways, and disorders are detected. TF programs were not the main focus and may be incompletely resolved.

## Evidence pattern
Entity definition: chromatin contacts were categorized as promoter-enhancer, promoter-promoter, promoter-ATAC, and other interactions in microglia, neurons, and oligodendrocytes. Comparison design: disease heritability and H-MAGMA gene associations were compared across three human brain cell types and across Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and schizophrenia. Statistical unit: GWAS variants, LD blocks or SNP annotations for stratified LD score regression, chromatin contact regions, and genes assigned by H-MAGMA. Effect metric: heritability enrichment in cell-type-specific chromatin contact annotations and overrepresentation of disease-risk genes and pathways by cell type. Boundary conditions: findings are based on cortical PLAC-seq-derived enhancer-promoter maps and GWAS summary statistics rather than direct perturbation or disease-state single-cell validation.

## Extends or contradicts
This paper extends the general finding that noncoding disease-associated variants can be interpreted through cell-type-specific enhancer-promoter maps by applying human microglial, neuronal, and oligodendrocyte 3D chromatin contacts to neurodegenerative and psychiatric GWAS. ## Boundary conditions
Works when: Works when disease-associated variants are primarily noncoding and can be evaluated using GWAS summary statistics together with matched cell-type-specific chromatin contact maps. Most applicable to human cortical microglia, neurons, and oligodendrocytes with available enhancer/promoter annotations such as H3K4me3, H3K27ac, and ATAC-seq, and to disorders with sufficiently powered GWAS datasets such as Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and schizophrenia.
Fails when: May fail or be incomplete when relevant regulatory programs occur in cell types not profiled, such as astrocytes, endothelial cells, peripheral immune cells, dopaminergic neurons, motor neurons, or disease-state microglial subtypes. May not capture enhancer-promoter wiring specific to aged, diseased, region-specific, or stimulated brain states if those states differ from the cortical samples used. Predictions can be wrong when chromatin contact does not imply functional regulation, when enhancer effects are context-dependent, or when GWAS power and ancestry composition are insufficient for reliable locus-to-gene assignment.
