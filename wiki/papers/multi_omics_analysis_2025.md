## Summary
This paper identifies cell-type-specific and bulk-level druggable targets for Alzheimer's disease through a multi-omics analysis integrating GWAS and eQTL datasets. It highlights novel candidate causal genes and supports drug repurposing efforts.

## Hypothesis framed
To identify cell-type-specific and bulk-level druggable targets for Alzheimer's disease (AD) using integrated multi-omics analysis.

## Questions answered
- What are the novel candidate causal genes for Alzheimer's disease identified through multi-omics analysis?
- How do the contributions of different brain cell types compare in the context of Alzheimer's disease?

## Key findings
A total of 28 candidate causal genes were identified, with 12 uniquely detected at the cell-type level, 9 exclusive to the bulk level, and 7 found in both. PABPC1 was highlighted as a novel candidate causal gene in astrocytes.

## Methods used
Integrated brain cell type-level and bulk-level eQTL datasets with AD GWAS datasets using Summary Data-Based Mendelian Randomization (SMR) and Bayesian Colocalization (COLOC).

## Method and dataset
Used SMR and COLOC on five AD GWAS datasets, two single-cell eQTL datasets, and one bulk eQTL dataset, approximately consisting of thousands of genetic variants with a focus on European ancestry.

## Limitations
The study predominantly focuses on individuals of European ancestry, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, potential biases in available datasets may prevent the discovery of all relevant genetic variants.

## Evidence pattern
The paper utilized comparison design leveraging effect metrics from integrated eQTL and GWAS datasets.
