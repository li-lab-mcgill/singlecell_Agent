## Summary
This study utilizes single nucleus multiomics to analyze cortical tissues from donors with Alzheimer's disease to identify cis-regulatory elements linked to transcriptional changes. The identification of ZEB1 and MAFB as regulatory factors provides new insights into AD-specific gene regulation.

## Hypothesis framed
ZEB1 and MAFB are candidate transcription factors that regulate gene expression in Alzheimer's disease.

## Questions answered
- What are the candidate cis-regulatory elements involved in Alzheimer's disease?
- How do ZEB1 and MAFB influence gene regulation in AD tissues?

## Key findings
The study identified 319,861 significant links between gene expression and accessible chromatin regions, with 40,831 unique to Alzheimer's disease. ZEB1 and MAFB were confirmed as candidate transcription factors affecting AD-specific regulation in neurons and microglia.

## Methods used
Single nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) and single nucleus ATAC sequencing (snATAC-seq).

## Method and dataset
Single nucleus multiomics on 105,332 nuclei from cortical tissues of 7 Alzheimer's disease and 8 control donors, focusing on regulatory landscapes.

## Limitations
The number of donors and specific brain regions examined may limit the generalizability of the findings.
