## Summary
This study integrates single-cell epigenomic and transcriptomic profiles from 3.5 million cells to analyze Alzheimer's disease progression and cognitive resilience across multiple brain regions. It identifies over 1 million candidate cis-regulatory elements and epigenomic changes associated with AD, enhancing the understanding of cognitive decline mechanisms.

## Hypothesis framed
The study aims to investigate epigenomic and transcriptomic dynamics in Alzheimer's disease progression and their relationship to cognitive resilience.

## Questions answered
- What epigenomic changes occur during Alzheimer's disease progression?
- How do these changes relate to cognitive resilience?

## Key findings
Identified over 1 million candidate cis-regulatory elements and regulatory modules; revealed epigenome relaxation and specific erosion signatures in brain regions related to AD progression, linking these to cognitive impairment and resilience.

## Methods used
Single-cell epigenomic and transcriptomic profiling.

## Method and dataset
Single-cell profiling on postmortem brain samples from 111 individuals (57 controls, 33 early-stage AD, 21 late-stage AD), analyzing 3.5 million cells to identify cis-regulatory elements.

## Limitations
Cohort was predominantly White, potentially limiting generalizability; focused on postmortem samples, which may not fully represent living individuals' AD dynamics.
