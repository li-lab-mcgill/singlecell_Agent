## Summary
This study investigates the role of transposable elements (TEs) in Alzheimer's disease (AD) by integrating single-nucleus RNA sequencing data from AD patients and controls with chromatin accessibility profiles. It reveals significant TE activation and dysregulation, especially in specific cell types such as excitatory neurons and oligodendrocytes.

## Hypothesis framed
Transposable elements contribute to the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease.

## Questions answered
- How does TE expression differ between Alzheimer's disease and control samples?
- What are the cell type-specific patterns of TE dysregulation in AD?

## Key findings
Identified 508 differentially expressed TE loci, with 84.3% upregulated in AD. Dysregulated TEs were mainly present in excitatory neurons (319 loci) and oligodendrocytes (165 loci), with SINE and LINE elements being predominant.

## Methods used
Integrated analysis of single-nucleus RNA sequencing and ATAC-seq chromatin accessibility data.

## Method and dataset
Analysis method involved integrating single-nucleus RNA sequencing from 11 AD patients and 7 controls, alongside ATAC-seq data, to assess TE expression in various cell types.

## Limitations
The study relies on publicly available data and may not encompass all cellular contexts; further investigation is needed to establish causative roles of TEs in AD pathology.

## Evidence pattern
The study utilized comparison design with controls and covariates, identifying cell-type specific patterns of dysregulated TEs.

Works when: Works when integrated datasets from single-nucleus RNA-seq and ATAC-seq are available across multiple AD patients.
