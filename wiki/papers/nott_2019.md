## Summary
This study defined cell type-specific noncoding regulatory regions and enhancer-promoter interactome maps for major human brain cell types to interpret brain disease-associated noncoding variants. It found that sporadic Alzheimer's disease risk variants are largely localized to microglia enhancers, while psychiatric disorder variants are mainly associated with neuronal enhancers and promoters, and experimentally validated a microglia-specific enhancer controlling BIN1 expression.

## Hypothesis framed
Common noncoding variants associated with brain diseases act through cell type-specific enhancers and promoters, with sporadic Alzheimer's disease risk variants preferentially affecting microglial enhancer-promoter regulatory networks.

## Questions answered
- Are sporadic Alzheimer's disease-associated noncoding variants enriched in regulatory elements of specific human brain cell types?
- Do enhancer-promoter interactome maps connect Alzheimer's disease risk variants in cell type-specific enhancers to candidate target genes in microglia?
- Does a microglia-specific enhancer containing Alzheimer's disease risk variants regulate BIN1 expression in a cell type-specific manner?

## Key findings
Psychiatric disorder-associated variants were primarily associated with neuronal enhancers and promoters, whereas sporadic Alzheimer's disease variants were largely confined to microglia enhancers. Enhancer-promoter interactome maps connected disease-risk variants in cell type-specific enhancers to promoters and identified an expanded microglial gene network implicated in Alzheimer's disease. Deletion of a microglia-specific enhancer harboring Alzheimer's disease risk variants ablated BIN1 expression in microglia but did not affect BIN1 expression in neurons or astrocytes.

## Methods used
Cell type-specific mapping of human brain enhancers and promoters, construction of enhancer-promoter interactome maps, overlap/enrichment analysis of brain disease-associated noncoding variants with cell type-specific regulatory elements, inference of target promoters and gene networks for disease-risk enhancers, and functional deletion of an Alzheimer's disease risk enhancer near BIN1 followed by cell type-specific expression assessment in microglia, neurons, and astrocytes.

## Method and dataset
The study used regulatory element and enhancer-promoter interaction maps from major human brain cell types together with common noncoding genetic variants associated with psychiatric disorders and sporadic Alzheimer's disease. Experimental validation focused on deletion of a microglia-specific enhancer containing Alzheimer's disease risk variants near BIN1 and comparison of BIN1 expression effects across microglia, neurons, and astrocytes. Sample sizes, exact assay platforms, and the complete number of regulatory elements or interactions are not provided in the summary. The analysis assumes that cell type-specific enhancer/promoter annotations and enhancer-promoter contacts can prioritize the genes and cell types through which noncoding GWAS variants act.

## Limitations
The summary does not provide detailed methodological information, sample sizes, or the full scope of validation experiments. Most enhancer-promoter links and disease associations appear to be based on genomic mapping and statistical enrichment, which prioritize candidate regulatory mechanisms but do not prove causality for every implicated variant or gene. Functional validation is specifically described for one microglia-specific enhancer near BIN1, so additional experiments are needed to confirm other predicted Alzheimer's disease enhancer-target links and gene network effects.

## Evidence pattern
Entity definition: major human brain cell type-specific enhancers, promoters, enhancer-promoter contacts, and disease-risk noncoding variants were defined as analysis entities. Comparison design: disease-associated variants for psychiatric disorders and sporadic Alzheimer's disease were compared for enrichment across neuronal versus microglial regulatory elements. Statistical unit: noncoding disease-associated variants and cell type-specific regulatory elements/interactions. Metric: enrichment/localization of disease variants in cell type-specific enhancers and promoters, plus enhancer-promoter connectivity to candidate target genes. Validation: deletion of a microglia-specific enhancer containing Alzheimer's disease risk variants near BIN1 tested whether the element controls BIN1 expression. Boundary conditions: causal experimental evidence is shown for the BIN1 enhancer in microglia, neurons, and astrocytes, while other network links remain predicted.

## Extends or contradicts
The study extends prior noncoding GWAS interpretation by assigning brain disease risk variants to specific human brain cell type regulatory elements and enhancer-target gene networks. It supports and expands the view that sporadic Alzheimer's disease genetic risk acts largely through microglial regulatory programs rather than primarily neuronal regulatory elements.

## Boundary conditions
Works when: The findings apply when interpreting common noncoding variants for brain diseases using human brain cell type-specific enhancer/promoter maps and enhancer-promoter interaction data. The strongest experimental support applies to a microglia-specific enhancer near BIN1 containing Alzheimer's disease risk variants, where deletion can be tested across microglia, neurons, and astrocytes.
Fails when: The approach does not by itself establish causal variants within each enhancer, prove every predicted enhancer-promoter link, or measure disease-state chromatin changes between Alzheimer's disease and control brains. It is limited when regulatory maps are missing for relevant cell states, rare variants, disease-induced chromatin states, or cell types not represented among the profiled major brain cell types.
