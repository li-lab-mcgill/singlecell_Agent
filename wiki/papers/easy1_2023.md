## Summary
This study introduces EasySci, a novel strategy for single-cell analysis, enabling the exploration of age-dependent cellular dynamics associated with aging and Alzheimer's disease. By profiling over 1.5 million single-cell transcriptomes, it identifies over 300 cell subtypes and their specific responses to aging and AD pathology.

## Hypothesis framed
EasySci can effectively reveal age-dependent cellular dynamics and molecular signatures related to Alzheimer's disease in human and mouse brains.

## Questions answered
- What rare cell types are associated with aging and Alzheimer's disease?
- How do single-cell responses differ in aging and Alzheimer's pathogenesis?

## Key findings
The study identified over 300 cell subtypes, noted depletion of rare progenitor cells, and differentiation of oligodendrocytes linked to aging and Alzheimer's disease pathology.

## Methods used
Single-cell combinatorial indexing strategy, transcriptomic profiling, and chromatin accessibility analysis.

## Method and dataset
The study utilized EasySci on 1.5 million single-cell transcriptomes and 400,000 chromatin accessibility profiles from mouse brains, alongside 118,240 human brain single-cell transcriptomes.

## Limitations
Findings are constrained by low sequencing depth, potential biases in sample selection, and complexities in dissecting interactions among identified cell types.
